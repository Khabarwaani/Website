Act 01 · Why We Are Building

The Old Newsroom Broke Down. The New Feed Is Drowning in Noise.

The mobile generation didn't stop caring about the world — they stopped tolerating legacy news formats that waste their time, insult their intelligence, or feed them manufactured outrage. Four converging realities explain why Khabarwaani exists:

01 · The Shift to Short Video

Built for the New Generation

Over 350M+ Indians consume short video daily, with 72%+ of Gen Z and younger Millennials discovering news primarily on vertical feeds (Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, regional video apps). Dense print broadsheets and 40-minute TV broadcasts no longer fit modern attention. If news isn't 10 to 60 seconds, visual, and concise, the audience simply scrolls past.

02 · The Misinformation Crisis

Fighting the Viral Fake News Epidemic

As news migrated to social feeds, truth got left behind. Feeds and chat groups are flooded with unverified WhatsApp forwards, sensational clickbait, and synthetic deepfakes. While 65%+ consume short video news, fewer than 20% trust social commentary. Khabarwaani bridges this trust deficit by bringing rigorous, primary-source journalism directly to the scroll.

03 · The Multilingual Reality

Bharat Speaks in Many Voices

India is not an English-monolith. Over 90% of new internet users discover content in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, and regional languages. Yet quality, high-budget explainers remain concentrated in English or Delhi-centric Hindi. Regional audiences are left with low-budget dubs or unvetted forwards. Verifiable truth must never be a single-language privilege.

04 · The Good Media Bias

Balanced Truth Over Manufactured Outrage

Legacy news channels survive on sensationalist bias: cherry-picking extreme clips, pitting guests against each other, and polarizing viewers to capture attention. Khabarwaani champions “good media bias”: radical transparency, explicit primary citations, and presenting multi-stakeholder perspectives calmly. We explain what happened, why it matters, and what both sides argue.

Act 02 · How We Are Building

Replacing Manufactured Anger with Automated, High-Craft Reel Making

For decades, cable TV news relied on manufactured outrage because yelling panels are cheap to produce, while investigative, beautifully crafted video is punishingly expensive. We are inverting that equation with an agentic newsroom engine.

Traditional Cable News Manufactured Outrage

Built on the television business model: weaponizing conflict, fear, and theatrical panel arguments to capture eyeballs.

  • 8 panelists yelling simultaneously across split-screen boxes
  • Hyperventilating breaking-news chyrons and manufactured hysteria
  • Emotional manipulation designed to provoke anger rather than insight
  • High production overhead: physical studios, satellite vans, and anchors
  • Low completion: younger viewers abandon the screen in minutes

The Khabarwaani Method Calm, Verified Clarity

Built for mobile understanding: high-density visual explainers that deliver context, balance, and dignity to the viewer.

  • Calm, natural narrative voice that respects the viewer's intelligence
  • One useful takeaway per reel, anchored by primary data citations
  • Data-rich motion graphics, spatial maps, and verified visual evidence
  • 10s–60s vertical format engineered for high retention and comprehension
  • Balanced perspective: explaining complex issues from all sides
The Production Bottleneck

Why High-Quality Reel Making Was Impossible at Scale

Producing a single 60-second animated news reel traditionally requires an exhausting, multi-day assembly line: an investigative researcher (6 hrs), a scriptwriter (3 hrs), an After Effects animator (8 hrs), a video editor in Premiere (6 hrs), and a voice artist (3 hrs). Total turnaround: 24 to 48 hours. By the time a traditional reel is rendered, the breaking news cycle is already over. Scaling that to 5 Indian languages multiplied costs by 5x.

The Solution

The Agentic Reel-Making Engine

Khabarwaani automates the friction of video editing by orchestrating specialized AI agents alongside human editors:

Agent 01

Fact-Auditing Mesh

Monitors primary gazettes, court filings, official statistics, and verified wires — verifying claims before scripting begins.

Agent 02

Script Reasoning

Transforms dense policy into tight 10s–60s narrative structures with clear psychological hooks, zero filler, and fair balance.

Agent 03

Automated Scene Composer

Replaces manual timeline editing by translating script directives into camera blocking, visual assets, and 9:16 vertical staging.

Agent 04

Vernacular Synthesizer

Generates expressive neural narration across Indian languages, matching regional dialects, authentic cadence, and cultural nuance.

Agent 05

Closed-Loop QA & Assembly

Validates lip-sync, eye-lines, and subtitles. If an issue is flagged, it re-renders only that 4-second clip before human editorial sign-off.

The Scaling Equation

Human Cost Multiplies with Every Language. Software Does Not.

A single verified story is cheap to report once. The cost explodes when legacy newsrooms try to re-shoot, re-write, and re-voice that same story for every language. Khabarwaani breaks this multiplication: people establish the facts and editorial standard once; technology carries the vernacular adaptation.
